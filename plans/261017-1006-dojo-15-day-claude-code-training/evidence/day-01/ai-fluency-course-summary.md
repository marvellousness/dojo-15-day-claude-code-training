## Big picture
- **AI Fluency** = the ability to work with AI in ways that are **effective, efficient, ethical and safe**.
- It rests on **four competencies, the 4Ds**: Delegation, Description, Discernment, Diligence.
- The 4Ds apply across **three modes of engagement**: Automation, Augmentation, Agency.
- The aim is a lasting framework, not tips and tricks that go out of date as models change.
- Theme throughout: AI is "an interactive partner, not a database or vending machine". It is powerful but not magic, and only as useful and safe as the way you engage with it.

```
            Automation      Augmentation       Agency
            (AI does task)  (think together)   (AI acts for you)
Delegation  ───────────────────────────────────────────────  what / who
Description ───────────────────────────────────────────────  how to ask
Discernment ───────────────────────────────────────────────  is it good?
Diligence   ───────────────────────────────────────────────  am I responsible?
```

## Lesson-by-lesson

### L1 · Introduction to AI Fluency (15 min)
- The course is about **collaborating** with AI, not learning AI as a technology.
- Outcomes: a framework for thoughtful AI use, confidence about when and how to use AI, practical collaboration skills, and the ability to evaluate and share AI-assisted work responsibly.
- It needs a shift in mindset as well as skills.
- Exercise: get access to any LLM (a free claude.ai account is enough). Reflect: past problems with AI, hopes for the course.

### L2 · Why do we need AI Fluency?
- Fluency = practical skills + knowledge + insights + values for working with AI while the technology changes fast.
- **Three modes:**
  - **Automation**: AI completes specific tasks following your instructions.
  - **Augmentation**: you and AI are partners in creative thinking and doing the task.
  - **Agency**: you configure AI to work independently for you. You shape its *knowledge and behaviour patterns* rather than hand it single tasks.
- (L10 refers to an exercise here, "Explore something you love", which picks a topic you know well and is reused later.)

### L3 · The 4D Framework (video 5 min)
| D | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Delegation** | Decide what work to do with AI and what to do yourself |
| **Description** | Communicate clearly and effectively with AI |
| **Discernment** | Evaluate AI outputs and behaviour critically |
| **Diligence** | Make sure you interact with AI responsibly |
- Exercise "Apply the 4Ds": pick a scenario (marketing emails, dataset research or story characters) and answer one question per D. Example for research: how to split the analysis, what context the AI needs, how to verify accuracy, what ethics apply when publishing.
- Reflect: which D is strongest for you and which is weakest.

### L4 · Generative AI fundamentals (Deep Dive 1, video 6 min)
- Generative AI **creates new content** instead of only analysing existing data.
- LLMs became possible through three things: the **transformer architecture** (algorithmic breakthrough), **vast training data** and **powerful compute**.
- Two learning stages: **pre-training** (patterns across billions of examples) and **fine-tuning** (learning to follow instructions and be helpful).
- Also introduced: **context windows** and **emergent capabilities**.

### L5 · Capabilities & limitations (video 7 min)
- **Capabilities:** works across many language tasks, keeps conversational flow, switches tasks without retraining, can connect to external tools.
- **Limitations:** knowledge cutoff, **hallucinations** (factually incorrect outputs), context window limits, trouble with complex reasoning.
- The field moves fast. Best results **combine strengths**: humans bring critical thinking, judgment, creativity and ethical oversight.
- Reflect: how does knowing how training works change how you use AI? What ethical issues does it raise?

### L6 · A closer look at Delegation (20 min, video 6 min)
- Delegation = decide what to do **yourself**, what to do **with AI**, and what AI can do **independently**, then share out the work.
- **Problem Awareness**: be clear on goals and the nature of the work *before* bringing in AI.
- **Platform Awareness**: know what the different AI systems can and can't do.
- **Task Delegation**: split work so each side plays to its strengths.
- It needs **domain expertise plus an understanding of AI**. The goal is the best human–AI partnership for each task, not automating everything.
- Exercise: pick a small task (an email, an outline, a meeting plan). With Claude, work out the vision, what good looks like, the pieces of work, and which pieces need human judgment or creativity. Have a real back-and-forth, then write a delegation plan.

### L7 · Project planning and Delegation (text lesson)
- Choose a **course project** that is substantial (several task types), doable in about 1 hour and that you care about. Types: communication, research, creative, learning.
- Step 2: tell Claude the idea and let it **ask you questions** until the vision and the success criteria are clear.
- Step 3: break it into major tasks. For each one: what skills or AI capabilities it needs, which human strengths it uses, where AI helps, and where collaboration has the most impact. Challenge assumptions as you go.
- **Save the plan.** It's reused for Description, Discernment and Diligence later.

### L8 · A closer look at Description (15 min, video 4 min)
- Description means setting up a **collaborative environment**, which is more than writing prompts. "AI can't read your mind."
| Type | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **Product Description** | *What* to create: outputs, format, audience, style |
| **Process Description** | *How* the AI should approach it: method, steps, frameworks |
| **Performance Description** | *How the AI behaves* while working with you: concise or detailed, challenging or supportive |
- Clear communication up front saves time.
- Exercise **"Bad Prompt Makeover"**: Claude gives you bad prompts and you fix them using all three types. Then swap: you write bad prompts for Claude to fix, and note what it adds.
- Reflect: which of the three types do you tend to leave out?

### L9 · Effective prompting techniques (Deep Dive 2)
- Prompt engineering = "designing effective instructions for AI systems": human communication principles plus considerations specific to AI.
- **Six foundational techniques:**
  1. **Give context**: what you want, why, and the background.
  2. **Show examples**: demonstrate the style or format you want.
  3. **Specify constraints**: format, length and other requirements.
  4. **Break complex tasks into steps**: walk it through multi-step reasoning.
  5. **Ask the AI to think first**: give it room to reason before answering.
  6. **Define the AI's role or tone**: how it should communicate.
- **"Secret weapon":** ask the AI to help improve your prompt.
- Prompting is **iterative** and can be done *with* the AI.
- Patterns that work: a clear task overview, a format spec, explicit constraints, relevant background.

### L10 · A closer look at Discernment (20 min)
- Discernment = thoughtfully evaluating AI **outputs, processes and behaviours**. It's "the flip side of Description" and keeps human judgment in charge.
| Type | What to check |
|---|---|
| **Product Discernment** | Quality of the output: **accuracy, appropriateness, coherence, relevance** |
| **Process Discernment** | How it approached the task: **logical errors, attention gaps, inappropriate reasoning** |
| **Performance Discernment** | How it behaved: did the communication style suit your needs? |
- Even the most advanced AI benefits from human oversight.
- Exercise **"Expert Discernment"**: on a topic you know well, ask for 3 different explanations. Judge each one on product, process and performance. Name the strongest and the weakest and say why, then build an improved version with Claude. Reflect on how much domain knowledge makes discernment possible, and how someone without it would struggle.
- Reflect: what signals that an output needs a closer look?

### L11 · The Description–Discernment loop (text, 10 min)
- Apply both skills to your course project. For **each task**, loop:
  1. **Describe**: the product, the process and the performance you want.
  2. **Discern**: judge the output, the approach and the behaviour.
  3. **Refine**: give feedback, adjust the description, ask for another version.
  4. **Integrate**: add your own expertise, decide what to keep, change or drop, and **own the final output**.
- Goal: results better than either human or AI could produce alone.
- Reflect: which descriptions worked best? Which took more effort, describing or discerning?

### L12 · A closer look at Diligence (20 min)
- The other three Ds are mainly about effectiveness and efficiency. Diligence covers **ethics and safety**, which the course treats as equally important.
| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Creation Diligence** | Be thoughtful about which AI systems you use and how you work with them (data shared, privacy, security) |
| **Transparency Diligence** | Be honest about AI's role with everyone who needs to know |
| **Deployment Diligence** | Verify and vouch for the AI-assisted outputs you use or share |
- Expectations differ between personal, academic and professional contexts. You're responsible for knowing which apply.
- Exercise **"Diligence Statement"** (~14 min): reflect on each of the three types for your project, then draft a statement with Claude. It names the AI used, what AI contributed, how you reviewed the work, that you take responsibility for the result, and any context-specific notes. Attach it as a footer, appendix or metadata.

### L13 · Conclusion (30 min)
- A recap of the 4Ds across the three modes. Fluency comes from **practice**, not overnight.
- Your expertise and judgment stay the foundation. The strongest outcomes come when human and AI build on each other's strengths.
- Exercise: talk with Claude about the course. Ask how the 4Ds apply to your job, and which Ds you seem strong or weak in. Share an exercise you did and ask for feedback.

### L14 · Course quiz
- 10 questions, ~8 min, **≥80% to pass**. Topics: what AI Fluency means, the 4Ds, the parts of each D (e.g. Problem Awareness). After passing, choose **"Create your completion badge"**.

### Additional activities (optional)
1. **Personal AI Fluency Plan**: rate yourself on each D (novice, developing or confident), pick 1–2 to focus on, plan activities, a timeline and how you'll measure progress.
2. **Prompt & Pattern Library**: 5–10 recurring tasks, each with a template prompt and placeholders. Record the Description techniques and Discernment checks that work.
3. **Game Night**: swap riddles, cooperative crosswords, word association (practise the three kinds of discernment), Twenty Questions, collaborative storytelling, RPG, concepts and constraints, and puzzles such as Project Euler.
